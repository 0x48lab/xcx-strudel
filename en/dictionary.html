Block dictionary

When do you use this block? How do you use it? Find out here.
Strudel for Xcratch ・ Tutorial ・ Rhythm book

This dictionary shows how to use each block, from the top of the palette down. Build the blocks as in the pictures and try them. "Open all the examples" opens a project with all the examples of the dictionary.

The names of the blocks and ▼ menus are the names of the functions and sounds of Strudel. Open "Name and history" to see where each name comes from.

The blocks in "play on track [ ]", from top to bottom, become the steps such as .fast(4) of Strudel.

Where do Strudel's names come from?

Strudel is based on TidalCycles, a programming language for music. Alex McLean from the UK made it around 2009 in a language called Haskell. People all over the world use it for "live coding": playing music by changing the code while it runs.

In 2022, Felix Roos and Alex McLean moved the ideas of TidalCycles into JavaScript, so it can play music right in your web browser. That is Strudel. So many names, like fast rev every jux, come from TidalCycles. Counting time in "cycles" also comes from the name TidalCycles.

Sound names (like bd lpf sawtooth) are even older. They come from the history of drum machines and synthesizers.

Getting started

show blocks for [ ] Beginner

What does it do?

Chooses which blocks are in the palette by level. Beginner shows a few, Normal adds mini-notation and effects, and Master shows all the blocks.

Try it

Click the green flag, and the Normal blocks appear in the palette.

When to use it

  • Put it at the start of a project so that the blocks it needs are in the palette

In Strudel

Strudel does not have it: it is a block of this extension.

Name and history

Strudel does not have it: it is a block of this extension, so that you can add blocks little by little and not get lost.

Tracks and tempo

play on track [ ] $: Beginner

What does it do?

Makes a pattern from the blocks in it and keeps playing it on a named track. Play the same name again, and it changes at the next cycle. Use another name, and the tracks play together.

Try it

The kick keeps playing: boom boom boom boom.

When to use it

  • Put drums, bass and melody on separate tracks, to stop or change them one by one
  • Play the same track with another pattern when a key is pressed, to switch the music

In Strudel

s("bd").fast(4)
Name and history

The blocks in it are read from the top, and each becomes one step of Strudel, such as .fast(4). One track is one $: line of Strudel.

stop track [ ] hush Beginner

What does it do?

Stops the named track. The other tracks keep playing.

Try it

Press space, and only the track "drums" stops.

When to use it

  • Stop the drums for a break (rhythm book)

In Strudel

$: s("bd*4").hush()
Name and history

In Strudel, delete the line, or add .hush() (be quiet) at its end.

stop all tracks hush Beginner

What does it do?

Stops and forgets all the tracks. The red stop button only stops the sound and keeps the tracks (they stay in "Strudel code of all tracks"); this block forgets them too.

Try it

Press 0, and all the sound stops.

When to use it

  • Use it at the end of a song, or to start again

In Strudel

hush()
Name and history

Hush means "shh, be quiet". In strudel.cc, Ctrl + . stops the sound too.

set tempo to [ ] BPM setcpm Beginner

What does it do?

Sets the tempo (beats in a minute). Usually about 120: hip hop is about 90, drum and bass about 170.

Try it

Click the green flag, and the tempo becomes 90: slower.

When to use it

  • Choose a tempo for the genre (the rhythm book has the BPM of each genre)

In Strudel

setcpm(90/4)
Name and history

BPM = beats per minute. Strudel counts cycles, so with 4 beats in a cycle it writes setcpm(90/4) (22.5 cycles a minute).

set beats in a cycle to [ ] setcpm Normal

What does it do?

Sets how many beats a cycle has. Usually 4. Set 3 for three beats, like a waltz.

Try it

It plays in three: boom chick chick.

When to use it

  • Make a waltz or a song in 6/8
  • Change how often "when a beat comes" runs

In Strudel

setcpm(120/3)
$: s("bd sd sd")
Name and history

The tempo (BPM) stays, and the length of a cycle changes. "When a beat comes" and "beat" follow this number.

Choosing sounds

s [ ] s Beginner

What does it do?

Chooses the kind of sound. In Beginner you choose it with ▼; from Normal you can write mini-notation. At the top of a track it sets the sound; below, it changes the sound (.s()).

Try it

It plays boom tick clap tick.

When to use it

  • Write a drum rhythm (rhythm book)
  • Put it below note to play the notes with a piano or a synth

In Strudel

s("bd hh sd hh")

Names in ▼

bd sd hh oh cp rim lt mt ht cr

Names of drum sounds. bd = bass drum, sd = snare, hh = hi-hat, oh = open hi-hat, cp = clap (hand clap), rim = rim, lt / mt / ht = low / middle / high tom, cr = crash cymbal.

s("bd hh sd hh")
Name and history

These short names come from the labels on drum machine knobs. They became popular as sound folder names in TidalCycles. bd = bass drum, sd = snare drum, hh = hi-hat.

sbd

A kick made by a synthesizer (synth bass drum). It is not a recorded sound. It is made by calculation.

s("sbd!4")
Name and history

The "boom" kick that slides down in pitch works the same way as the TR-808 kick: the pitch drops very quickly.

sine square sawtooth triangle

The basic wave shapes of a synthesizer. sine = soft, square = beepy, sawtooth = jagged and bright, triangle = clean.

note("c4").s("sawtooth")
Name and history

They are all names of wave shapes. sine is the sine wave from math, square is a square, sawtooth is "the teeth of a saw", and triangle is a triangle. They have been used since the analog synths of the 1960s.

supersaw

A thick, sparkly sound made by stacking many sawtooth waves, each a little out of tune.

note("[a3,c4,e4]").s("supersaw")
Name and history

The name of a feature on the Roland JP-8000 synth, which came out around 1997. Its sound of 7 stacked sawtooth waves became a typical sound of a kind of music called trance.

white

A "shhh" noise. Used for a riser, a sound that gets louder and louder in a build-up.

s("white*16")
Name and history

White noise. White light has all colors mixed in. In the same way, this noise has all pitches mixed in at about the same amount, so it is called "white".

Name and history

Short for sound. The name comes straight from SuperDirt, the sound engine of TidalCycles.

note [ ] note Beginner

What does it do?

Writes the pitch with a note name (c4) or a number (60 = c4). Put s below it to play it with that sound.

Try it

The piano plays C, E, G.

When to use it

  • Write a melody or a bass line
  • Write [c4,e4,g4] with commas to play a chord

In Strudel

note("c4 e4 g4").s("piano")
Name and history

"Note" means a musical note. The numbers are MIDI numbers (MIDI is a shared set of rules for electronic instruments, made in 1983). 60 is middle C.

n [ ] n Normal

What does it do?

Writes which note of a scale to play, by number. Put scale below it to turn the numbers into notes of that scale. 0 is the first. With numbers you never play a wrong note.

Try it

It plays C, E, G and the high C.

When to use it

  • Write a melody which stays in the scale
  • Change only the scale to make the same melody bright or dark

In Strudel

n("0 2 4 7").scale("C4:major").s("piano")
Name and history

n means number.

seq [ ] [ ] [ ] [ ] seq Beginner

What does it do?

Puts sounds in order inside one cycle. With 4 sounds, each one plays for 1/4 of a cycle.

Try it

It plays boom tick clap tick.

When to use it

  • Build a rhythm in Beginner. Choose ~ (rest) in ▼ for a step with no sound

In Strudel

seq(s("bd"), s("hh"), s("sd"), s("hh"))
Name and history

Short for sequence (a line of things in order). It is the same word as "sequencer", a machine that plays sounds in order. In mini-notation, put them in a row with spaces ("bd hh sd hh").

stack [ ] [ ] stack Beginner

What does it do?

Plays sounds at the same time, stacked up.

Try it

The kick and the hi-hat play together, 4 times.

When to use it

  • Play two sounds together in Beginner. To stack more, use the C block stack or separate tracks

In Strudel

stack(s("bd"), s("hh")).fast(4)
Name and history

It means "to pile up" in English. In mini-notation, a , (comma) does the same thing ("bd, hh").

stack (C block) stackBranch Normal

What does it do?

Plays the blocks in it as another chain, at the same time as the sound above. Blocks in it change only the sound in it; blocks below it change all the sounds.

Try it

The kick and the snare play together, with the sounds of the TR-909.

When to use it

  • Write each drum in its own short input, easy to read (rhythm book)

In Strudel

stack(s("bd*4"), s("[~ sd]*2")).bank("RolandTR909")
Name and history

One of these blocks is one part between the commas of stack( , ) in Strudel. Separate tracks can be stopped one by one; stacked sounds stop together.

cat [ ] [ ] cat Beginner

What does it do?

Plays them by turns, one per cycle. First c4, then g4, then c4 again...

Try it

It plays C, G, C, G by turns.

When to use it

  • Change the bass note every cycle

In Strudel

cat(note("c4"), note("g4")).fast(4)
Name and history

Short for concatenate (to join together). In programming, this name is often used for joining text. It is the same as < > in mini-notation.

sprite sound [ ] Normal

What does it do?

Plays a sound in the Sounds tab of the sprite (such as your recorded voice), like a drum.

Try it

The cat says meow twice.

When to use it

  • Make a beat with your own voice (tutorial step 10)

In Strudel

Strudel does not have it: it is a block of this extension.

Name and history

Strudel loads sound files with samples(). strudel.cc cannot read the sounds of a sprite, so they do not play in the exported code.

chord [ ] [ ] Master

What does it do?

Plays a chord from a root and a kind. major is bright, minor is dark, 7 and maj7 sound jazzy.

Try it

C, E, G and B play together.

When to use it

  • Put arp below it to play the notes one by one
  • Chop the chord with struct

In Strudel

note("[c4,e4,g4,b4]").s("piano")
Name and history

"Chord" comes from the same word as accord (agreement, harmony).

Changing the sound

The blocks from here go below a sound block in a track. They work from the top down.

fast [ ] fast Beginner

What does it do?

Makes the pattern N times faster. A single sound plays N times in one cycle.

Try it

The hi-hat ticks 8 times in a cycle.

When to use it

  • Make the hi-hat tick (8 for eighth notes, 16 for sixteenth notes)
  • Play a melody twice as fast

In Strudel

s("hh").fast(8)
Name and history

A basic name from TidalCycles. It is the same as * in mini-notation ("hh*8").

slow [ ] slow Normal

What does it do?

Makes the pattern N times slower, stretching it over N cycles.

Try it

The four notes play slowly, over two cycles.

When to use it

  • Change long pads and chords slowly

In Strudel

note("c4 e4 g4 b4").slow(2).s("piano")
Name and history

The opposite of fast. It is the same as / in mini-notation ("c4/2").

scale [ ] [ ] scale Normal

What does it do?

Turns the numbers of n into notes of the chosen scale. Choose the root (such as C4) and the kind of scale.

Try it

It goes up C, D, E♭, F, G: a dark sound.

When to use it

  • Keep the numbers and change the scale to change the mood

In Strudel

n("0 1 2 3 4").scale("C4:minor").s("piano")

Names in ▼

major minor

major is a bright scale (major key), minor is a dark scale (minor key).

n("0 2 4").scale("C4:minor")
Name and history

In English, major = "bigger", minor = "smaller". They are named by whether the gap between C and E (a third) is big or small.

major:pentatonic minor:pentatonic

A scale with only 5 notes. It sounds nice in any order.

n("0 1 2 3 4").scale("C4:major:pentatonic")
Name and history

From the Greek penta (5) + tonic (tone). The Japanese "yonanuki" scale, which leaves out the 4th and 7th notes, is in the same family.

blues dorian chromatic

blues is the scale of blues music, dorian is a scale loved in jazz and Celtic music, and chromatic is a scale with every half step.

n("0 1 2 3").scale("C4:dorian")
Name and history

dorian is a scale named after the Dorians of ancient Greece. It came down to us through the church music of the Middle Ages. chromatic comes from the Greek chroma (color).

Name and history

It comes from the Latin word scala, which means "ladder", because the notes line up like the steps of a ladder.

struct [ ] struct Normal

What does it do?

Chops the sound into the rhythm you write with x (play) and ~ (rest).

Try it

The chord plays on the rhythm: hit, rest, hit, hit.

When to use it

  • Put a chord on a rhythm, like a piano or guitar part

In Strudel

note("[c3,e3,g3]").struct("x ~ x x").s("piano")
Name and history

Short for structure (the frame or skeleton). It means you borrow just the "skeleton" of a rhythm.

beat [ ] [ ] beat Normal

What does it do?

Cuts a cycle into N steps and plays on the numbered steps. 0 is the first step. List the numbers with ,, and add ? like 7? to drop that step sometimes.

Try it

The kick plays off the beats and bounces.

When to use it

  • Write kicks and snares which play here and there (rhythm book)

In Strudel

s("bd").beat("0,7,10", 16)
Name and history

English for the pulse of music. Putting sounds on 16 steps is how drum machines such as the TR-808 build rhythms with their 16 step buttons.

bank [ ] bank Normal

What does it do?

Changes the drums to the sounds of the chosen drum machine.

Try it

The kick becomes the hard sound of the TR-909.

When to use it

  • Use the TR-909 for house and techno, the TR-808 for hip hop and trap

In Strudel

s("bd*4").bank("RolandTR909")
Name and history

From sample bank (a storehouse of samples). The TR-808 (1980) and TR-909 (1983) are Roland drum machines. These famous machines made the sound of hip-hop, house and techno.

effect [ ▼] [ ] Normal

What does it do?

Adds the effect chosen in ▼. The number can also be a mini-notation pattern or a wave.

Try it

The high part of the jagged bass is cut, and it becomes a muffled, fat sound.

When to use it

  • Muffle a bass, add room to the drums, make the hi-hat softer
  • Stack many effects on a sound (they work from the top)

In Strudel

note("c2*4").s("sawtooth").lpf(600)

Names in ▼

lpf hpf

lpf cuts the high sounds and makes it muffled. hpf cuts the low sounds and makes it thin and tinny. The number is where the cutting starts (Hz).

s("sawtooth").lpf(600)
Name and history

Low-pass filter (a filter that lets low sounds pass) and high-pass filter. The filters in the synths that Robert Moog made in the 1960s are famous.

lpq

Boosts the edge of the filter for a "wowww" sound with a strong character (resonance).

s("sawtooth").lpf(400).lpq(12)
Name and history

q is an electronics word: the "Q value" (how sharp the cut is).

lpenv lpd lps

Opens and closes the filter each time a sound plays. lpd is the time until it closes, lps is how much stays open. The "bwow" of acid music is this.

note("a1*8").s("sawtooth").lpf(200).lpenv(4)
Name and history

env = envelope (how the shape of a sound changes). d = decay, s = sustain (see ADSR below).

attack decay sustain release

The shape of the volume. attack = how slowly it starts, decay = time to go down, sustain = how loud it stays, release = how it fades out at the end.

note("c4").s("sine").attack(.5).release(2)
Name and history

The 4 first letters make "ADSR". It is said that Vladimir Ussachevsky, an electronic music researcher, came up with it in the 1960s. Almost every synth today has it.

room

Echoing space (reverb). It sounds like playing in a big room.

s("sd").room(.5)
Name and history

"Room" in English. The sound echoes differently depending on the size of the room.

delay delaytime delayfeedback

An echo. The same sound repeats a little later. delaytime is the gap, delayfeedback is how many times it repeats.

note("c4 e4").delay(.4).delaytime(.25)
Name and history

It means "being late" in English. Long ago, people made echoes by looping recording tape (like the Roland Space Echo, 1974).

gain

How loud the sound is. 1 is normal.

s("hh*8").gain(.6)
Name and history

In English it means "to increase" or "profit". It is a word for how many times bigger an electric signal is made.

pan

Left-right position. 0 is left, 0.5 is the middle, 1 is right.

s("hh*8").pan(sine.range(0, 1))
Name and history

From panorama (a wide view). It means where in the left-right view you put the sound.

crush

Makes the sound crunchy (a bitcrusher). The smaller the number, the rougher it gets.

s("bd sd").crush(4)
Name and history

It "crushes" the detail of the sound (the number of bits), so it is called crush. It sounds close to the 8-bit sounds of old game consoles.

distort

Distortion. Bends the sound to make it fat and rough.

note("a1").s("sawtooth").distort(2)
Name and history

The same as the distortion of an electric guitar. In the 1950s-60s, amps turned up too loud broke the sound, and people liked it as a cool sound.

speed

The playback speed of a recorded sound. 2 is twice as fast and higher. -1 plays it backwards.

s("bd").speed(2)
Name and history

It works the same way as spinning a record or tape faster to make the sound higher.

clip

The length of a sound. 1 lasts until the next sound, 0.3 cuts it short.

note("c4 e4").s("square").clip(.3)
Name and history

It means "to cut off" in English.

vowel

Makes the sound like a voice saying vowels such as "a, e, i, o, u".

s("sawtooth").vowel("<a e i o>")
Name and history

"Vowel" in English. It is a filter that copies the sounds (formants) that make the vowels of a human voice.

unison detune spread

How thick a supersaw is. unison is the number of stacked waves, detune is how far apart the pitches are, spread is how wide it is left to right.

note("a3").s("supersaw").unison(7).detune(.4).spread(.8)
Name and history

Unison means "one sound" in Latin. It is when many people sing the same note. Detune means "to put out of tune".

fm

Makes the sound metallic, like a bell or an electric piano (FM synthesis).

note("c4").s("sine").fm(2)
Name and history

Frequency modulation (the same words as in FM radio). John Chowning at Stanford University discovered it in 1967, and the Yamaha DX7 of 1983 spread it around the world.

orbit

Chooses the path (1-8) the sound goes through. Reverb, echo and duck work separately for each path.

note("a1*8").s("sawtooth").orbit(2)
Name and history

"Orbit" in English. It is the name of the system in SuperDirt, the sound engine of TidalCycles, that keeps each track's effects apart so they do not mix.

duck duckattack duckdepth

Each time the kick plays, the sounds on other paths (orbits) shrink. The sound "pumps" as if it is breathing.

s("bd*4").duck("2:3").duckattack(.2).duckdepth(.8)
Name and history

"Duck" means "to quickly lower your head". It comes from "ducking", a trick of turning the music down while someone talks on the radio. In club music it is also called "sidechain" or "pumping", and it was popular in 2000s French house and EDM.

Name and history

Each name in ▼ is a Strudel function. Look them up in the list below.

add [ ] add Normal

What does it do?

Adds a number to the pitch (number). You can add a pattern too, like <0 2 4>.

Try it

The same melody goes 2 steps up the scale in the next cycle, 4 steps up in the one after, and then back.

When to use it

  • Repeat a phrase at different heights

In Strudel

n("0 2 4").add(n("<0 2 4>")).scale("C4:major").s("piano")
Name and history

It means "to add" in English.

rev rev Normal

What does it do?

Plays the pattern backwards, from the end.

Try it

It plays G, E, D, C: from the end.

When to use it

  • Use it with every to play backwards now and then

In Strudel

note("c4 d4 e4 g4").rev().s("piano")
Name and history

Short for reverse.

ply [ ] ply Normal

What does it do?

Repeats each sound in the pattern N times.

Try it

Each sound plays twice: boom boom, clap clap.

When to use it

  • Make hi-hat rolls (trap) and fills

In Strudel

s("bd sd").ply(2)
Name and history

"Ply" means a layer or a twist of thread. Think of making one thread into many layers. The name comes from TidalCycles.

every [ ] [ ] every Normal

What does it do?

Changes the pattern in the ▼ way once every N cycles. ▼ has rev, fast 2, slow 2, ply 2, transpose 12 and transpose 7.

Try it

Once in four cycles, the melody plays backwards.

When to use it

  • Add a change now and then, so that the loop does not stay the same (fills)

In Strudel

note("c4 e4 g4").every(4, x => x.rev()).s("piano")
Name and history

It means "every" in English. One of the most famous functions, there since the early days of TidalCycles.

degradeBy [ ] % degradeBy Master

What does it do?

Leaves out sounds by chance. With 30, 30% of the sounds do not play.

Try it

Some hi-hats drop out, and it sounds like a person playing.

When to use it

  • Make fast hi-hats sparse to make a loop feel alive

In Strudel

s("hh*16").degradeBy(0.3)
Name and history

Degrade means "to make worse" or "to break down". It is the same as ? in mini-notation ("hh*16?").

sometimes [ ] sometimes Master

What does it do?

Changes the pattern in the ▼ way sometimes (on about half of the sounds).

Try it

Now and then a C plays an octave higher.

When to use it

  • Give a repeated note a different change each time

In Strudel

note("c4*8").sometimes(x => x.transpose(12)).s("piano")
Name and history

It means "sometimes" in English. Its friends are often and rarely.

euclid [ ] [ ] euclid Master

What does it do?

Spreads N sounds over M steps as evenly as possible. (3, 8) is a rhythm you can hear in music all over the world.

Try it

It plays in 3, 3, 2: tock, tock, tock.

When to use it

  • Stack it on four on the floor for a catchy offset (rhythm book)

In Strudel

s("rim").euclid(3, 8)
Name and history

Godfried Toussaint, a computational geometry researcher, showed in papers around 2004-2005 that traditional rhythms from Cuba and Africa can be made with the same steps as the "Euclidean algorithm". That is ancient Greek math from about 2300 years ago, for finding the greatest common divisor of two numbers. The original steps were used by Bjorklund, a physicist, to set the timing of a particle accelerator.

jux [ ] jux Master

What does it do?

Splits the sound into left and right, and changes only the right in the ▼ way. Left and right play different sounds, so it sounds wide.

Try it

The left plays as usual and the right plays backwards (use headphones).

When to use it

  • Make the sound wide

In Strudel

s("bd hh sd hh").jux(x => x.rev())
Name and history

Short for juxtapose (to put side by side). A name born in TidalCycles.

off [ ] [ ] off Master

What does it do?

Takes the pattern, delays it a little, changes it in the ▼ way, and stacks it on top.

Try it

G plays just after C, and B just after E.

When to use it

  • Add a shifted harmony, like an echo

In Strudel

note("c4 e4").off(0.125, x => x.transpose(7)).s("piano")
Name and history

It means "shifted" or "away" in English. The name comes from TidalCycles.

superimpose [ ] superimpose Master

What does it do?

Stacks a copy changed in the ▼ way on top of the original pattern.

Try it

C plays with the C an octave higher.

When to use it

  • Make a lead thicker

In Strudel

note("c4 e4").superimpose(x => x.transpose(12)).s("piano")
Name and history

It means "to lay one thing over another". It is the same word as the photo trick of showing two pictures at once (superimposing).

chop [ ] chop Master

What does it do?

Cuts one sound into N pieces and plays them in small bits.

Try it

The kick is cut into 4 pieces which play one after another.

When to use it

  • Cut a recorded voice and play the pieces (tutorial step 10)

In Strudel

s("bd").chop(4)
Name and history

It means "to cut" or "to chop" in English. Cutting recorded sounds into small pieces and rearranging them is a hip-hop trick that spread with the samplers of the 1980s.

transpose [ ] transpose Master

What does it do?

Moves the notes up by N half steps (down if N is negative). 12 is one octave.

Try it

C, E, G play an octave lower.

When to use it

  • Move a melody down to bass height

In Strudel

note("a1*16").s("sawtooth").lpf(100).lpenv(5.4).lps(0.2).lpd(0.12).lpq(2)
Name and history

The Roland TB-303 from 1981 was made to replace a bass player, but it did not sell. Then in 1987, Phuture in Chicago made "Acid Tracks" by turning its knobs round and round, and "acid house" was born. The name comes from the function acidenv in Switch Angel's strudel-scripts. It is exported as s, lpf, lpenv, lps, lpd and lpq.

Names in ▼

scope

Shows the shape of the sound wave as a line.

s("sawtooth")._scope()
Name and history

Short for oscilloscope, a tool for looking at electric waves.

pianoroll

Shows the pitch and length of notes as bars that flow sideways.

note("c4 e4 g4")._pianoroll()
Name and history

Around 1900, player pianos played songs from paper rolls with holes in them. The name comes from how those looked.

spectrum

Shows how much of each sound is playing, from low to high.

s("bd hh")._spectrum()
Name and history

"Spectrum" in English. Light going through a prism splits into rainbow colors. In the same way, this splits sound by pitch so you can see it.

punchcard

Shows the piano roll like a card with holes in it.

note("c4 e4 g4")._punchcard()
Name and history

A punch card is a card that stores information by where its holes are. In the early 1800s, the Jacquard loom used them to control the patterns of cloth. Later they were used in computers too.

Name and history

In Strudel, add ._pianoroll() or another one after the pattern.
